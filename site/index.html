      Overview
      
    Abstract & Core Research Question
    
    
    
    
    
      
    
        Recurrent language models repeatedly apply shared weights to a continuous hidden state before decoding. More recurrence does not guarantee recovery when an intermediate trajectory is unproductive, and independent branches do not exchange latent updates.
      
    
      
    
        TEBDİL asks whether a receiving trajectory can temporarily visit toward a donor state, execute the genuine shared recurrent transition there, and return the computed update without permanently switching trajectories. The receiving state remains the anchor.
      
    
      
    
        The recorded methods include fixed schedules, heuristic adaptive rules, and offline-trained scorers. The default learned controllers use hand-set margin and entropy eligibility gates; the explicitly ungated ablation disables them, and their action scores are not calibrated probabilities. At inference time these policies use recorded state features, not gold answers. Results below are exploratory: they show individual recoveries and failures, but do not establish a general advantage over parallel, mixing, or random controls. Experiment selectors, trace downloads, uncertainty intervals, cost warnings, and provenance expose what each artifact supports.
      
    
    
    
  
    

    



    
  
    
    
    
      Architecture & Formulation
      
    TEBDİL at a Glance
    
      
    
        Unlike multi-agent systems that pass text or train external adapter modules, TEBDİL operates
        entirely inside a single frozen looped language model by exploiting the nonlinear vector field
        of its shared recurrent transition operator $F_{\theta,t}$. Numeric values retained in the diagrams
        are labeled historical snapshots; the selected recording and provenance determine current evidence.
      
    
    
    

    
    
    
      
    
        Part 1
        
    Ouro Recurrence & Observed Perturbation Scope
    
      
    
      
    
        
    
          
    
            In ByteDance/Ouro-1.4B-Thinking, each Universal Transformer recurrent step $t \in \{0,1,2,3\}$
            passes the hidden state $H_t \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 2048}$ through all 24 shared decoder layers
            followed by final RMSNorm. Each decoder layer applies 4 Sandwich RMSNorm operations
            (input_layernorm, input_layernorm_2,
            post_attention_layernorm, post_attention_layernorm_2), totaling 96 layer-level RMSNorm normalizations, plus the final RMSNorm.
          
    
          
    
            
    
              Last-Token Scope ($\eta \le 0.05$)
              Historical nine-problem scope subset
            
    
            
    
              Perturbing only the final token leaves the prefix unchanged at the fork. In the historical nine-problem
              schedule/scope subset, $t_{\mathrm{visit}}=2$ last-token transfer records mean residual norm $0.944$ and accuracy $11.11\%$.
              This observation does not identify normalization or attention as its cause.
            
    
          
    
          
    
            
    
              Full-Sequence Scope ($\eta = 0.25$, $\tau = 0.35$)
              Sustained Recorded Branch Separation
            
    
            
    
              A full-sequence perturbation changes more than the final token. In the same nine-problem subset,
              $t_{\mathrm{visit}}=2$ full-scope transfer records mean residual norm $41.7568$ and accuracy $22.22\%$.
              These descriptive scope results are available in the schedule-ablation artifact below.
            
    
          
    
        
    

        
    
          
          
    
            Historical Token-Scope Contrast. Full-sequence scope records roughly $44\times$ the residual norm and two correct cases rather than one in the nine-problem subset. This changes scope and perturbation settings; it is not a causal test of normalization. Bars are schematic, not proportional.
          
    
        
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
    
      
    
        Part 2
        
    The Cooperative Latent Excursion Operator ($E_1$)
    
      
    
      
    
        
    
          
    
            Step A · Midpoint Probe
            Cross-Trajectory Interpolation at $t=2$
          
    
          
    
            Given primary state $H_t^A$ and perturbed donor state $H_t^B$, TEBDİL constructs a temporary visit state
            toward the donor. A trust-region clip may replace the requested $\alpha$ with an effective $\alpha_{\mathrm{eff}}$, after which the shared recurrent operator $F_{\theta,t}$ is evaluated:
          
    
          
    
            $$H_t^{\mathrm{visit}} = H_t^A + \alpha_{\mathrm{eff}}(H_t^B - H_t^A)$$
            $$V_t^{\mathrm{visit}} = F_{\theta,t}\!\left(H_t^{\mathrm{visit}},\; x,\; \mathcal{C}_t\right) - H_t^{\mathrm{visit}}$$
          
    
        
    

        
    
          
    
            Step B · Anchored Return
            Transfer the visited update, not the donor state
          
    
          
    
            The trust region clips the visit offset, producing $\alpha_{\mathrm{eff}}$; it does not normalize or clip the visited update.
            For full-scope, ungated transfer, the computed update returns to the receiving anchor with weight $\beta$:
          
    
          
    
            $$H_{t+1}^{A'} = H_t^A + V_t^A + \beta\big(V_t^{\mathrm{visit}}-V_t^A\big)$$
            
    Scoped, coordinate-restricted, and native-gated variants further mask or scale the transferred correction. Unstable interventions fall back to ordinary recurrence; inspect the selected trace.
    
          
    
        
    
      
    

      
    
        Why Return to $H_t^A$ Instead of Staying at $H_t^{\mathrm{visit}}$ ($C_0$)?
        In our Phase 3 ablation, staying at the visited state without returning ($C_0$: $H_{t+1}^{C_0} = H_t^{\mathrm{visit}} + V_t^{\mathrm{visit}}$)
        changes the receiving anchor rather than transporting an update. The recorded graph_validation_002 comparison can be inspected below.
        An anchored return is a distinct operator, not a guarantee of preserving answer correctness.
      
    
    
    

    
    
    
      
    
        Part 3
        
    Label-Free Adaptive Excursion Policy ($E_2$)
    
      
    
      
    
        
    
          
    
            Unconditional excursion ($E_1$) applies $\beta{=}0.5$ on every problem instance—even when Branch $A$ is already highly confident,
            risking unnecessary compute or over-excursion. Our adaptive policy $E_2$ projects intermediate state $H_2^A$ through the native
            readout head $g_\phi(H_2^A)$ at step $t{=}2$ and computes two strictly label-free uncertainty signals:
          
    
          
    
            $$m_t^A = z_{(1)}^A - z_{(2)}^A \quad (\text{Top-2 Logit Margin}), \qquad \mathcal{H}_t^A = -\sum_{v \in V} p_t^A(v)\log p_t^A(v)$$
          
    
          
    
            The current $E_2$ heuristic requires:
            (1) low margin and high entropy ($m_2^A \le 0.70$ and $\mathcal{H}_2^A \ge 1.20$), and
            (2) a finite, stable donor whose relative branch divergence lies inside the configured trust window $\frac{\|H_2^A - H_2^B\|_F}{\|H_2^A\|_F} \in [0.002,\; 0.65]$.
            Budget, stability, and donor checks can also reject a visit. Exact thresholds and decisions belong to each recording's manifest and trace, not a universal learned rule. Skipping the visit avoids that evaluation, not all donor cost.
          
    
        
    
        
    
          
    
            
            Runtime Label-Access Check & Recorded Cost
          
    
          
    
            Enforced by automated unit test tests/test_no_label_leakage.py:
            the core and controller runtime modules are scanned for forbidden gold-label and validator references.
            This check does not establish benchmark independence or exclude every possible leakage path.
          
    
          
    Recorded controller summaries load from the export manifest. See the results tables for uncertainty and provenance.
    
        
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
    
      
    
        Part 4
        
    Self-Directed Latent Relocation Controller ($\pi_\psi$: $E_3$, $D_{\text{nearest}}$, $E_1^{\text{subspace}}$)
    
      
    
      
    
        
    
          
    
            Beyond hand-tuned heuristics ($E_2$), TEBDİL trains a lightweight meta-controller $\pi_\psi(s_2)$ on counterfactual
            trajectory rollouts from an explicit training split. Training problem IDs, action counts, split checks, and checkpoint provenance are exposed in the exported counterfactual artifacts
            to predict the compute-penalized advantage over $\mathcal{A} = \{\text{CONTINUE}, \text{EXCURSION}(d, \alpha, \beta), \text{EXIT}\}$:
          
    
          
    
            $$J(s_t,a) = R_{\mathrm{task}}(s_t,a)-\lambda_{\mathrm{cost}}\frac{C(a)}{\max(1,C_{\mathrm{ref}})}$$
            
    Offline task reward combines correctness, bounded log-probability shaping, degradation penalties, and optional state/action shaping. These use gold labels only during counterfactual training; coefficients and shaping variants are recorded with the checkpoint.
    
          
    
          
    
            At test time, $\pi_\psi$ uses checkpoint-defined state-action features $\phi(s_t,a)$:
            current uncertainty, observed trajectory changes, donor statistics, budget/depth, and candidate intervention parameters.
            The trained scorer ranks eligible actions. Default gated variants add hand-set margin/entropy rules for EXIT and EXCURSION; the explicitly ungated ablation removes these rules. The gates are heuristic, not learned; inference does not read the gold answer.
          
    
        
    
        
    
          
    
            
            Historical Phase 5–8 Recorded Snapshots
          
    
          
    
            • 3-Branch Donor Selection (Phase 5): Compare D_nearest against other donor rules and the gold-informed D_oracle upper bound in the manifest-backed Phase 5 table. An oracle match on a small recording is not evidence of general optimality.
    
            • Coordinate-Restricted Transfer (Phase 6): E1_subspace restricts correction coordinates; it is not a learned SVD subspace. The recorded comparison is exploratory.
    
            • Learned MLP Controller (Phase 7–8): Inspect its recorded accuracy, rescues, degradations, and execution cost in the manifest-backed summary above. A learned scorer with heuristic gates is not an end-to-end learned policy; lower cost is not guaranteed.
          
    
        
    
      
    
    
    
  
    

    



    
  
    
    
    
      How Excursion-and-Return Differs from Linear Mixing
      
    Theoretical & Mechanistic Insights
    
      
    
        What mathematically distinguishes TEBDİL ($E_1$) from simply averaging the hidden states ($M_0$) or
        averaging the individual update vectors ($M_1$) of two trajectories?
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    
        
    
          
          Decomposition 1 — Nonlinear Update Residual
        
    
        
    
          Define the nonlinear excursion curvature residual $C_t$ at step $t$ as the deviation of the visited-state transition vector from the convex combination of endpoint transition vectors:
        
    
        
    
          $$C_t \;\triangleq\; V_t^{\mathrm{visit}} - \Big[(1-\alpha_{\mathrm{eff}})V_t^A + \alpha_{\mathrm{eff}}V_t^B\Big]$$
          $$H_{t+1}^{A'}(E_1) \;=\; \underbrace{H_t^A + (1-\alpha_{\mathrm{eff}}\beta)V_t^A + \alpha_{\mathrm{eff}}\beta V_t^B}_{\text{Linear Update Mixing } M_1(\gamma=\alpha_{\mathrm{eff}}\beta)} \;+\; \beta C_t$$
        
    
        
    
          Algebraic implication: For full-scope, ungated transfer in the donor direction, local affinity implies $C_t \equiv 0$ and equivalence to $M_1$ at the same effective $\alpha$. This is an exact-arithmetic statement; quantization can move the actual visit off the endpoint segment. The identity does not apply unchanged to random-direction, masked, projected, or gated variants. The graph case below contrasts recorded outputs; one case is not a causal proof.
        
    
      
    

      
    
        
    
          
          Control 2 — Directional Specificity of $C_t$
        
    
        
    
          Is the benefit of $C_t$ merely an artifact of injecting extra vector norm at step $t{=}2$? Phase 3 tests two directional controls, matched before scope/projection and final dtype casting, not necessarily at the applied output:
        
    
        
    
          $$E_1^{\text{rand-curv}}:\quad V_t^{\text{synth}} = \big[(1-\alpha_{\mathrm{eff}})V_t^A + \alpha_{\mathrm{eff}} V_t^B\big] + \frac{\|C_t\|_F}{\|u\|_F}\,u$$
          $$E_0:\quad H_t^{\text{visit,rand}} = H_t^A + \alpha_{\mathrm{eff}} \|H_t^B - H_t^A\|_F \frac{w}{\|w\|_F}$$
        
    
        
    
          Read the paired outcomes: The exported Phase 3 recording includes both rescues and degradations. Its fixed and randomized-residual methods have the same aggregate accuracy, despite different per-problem outcomes. Residual magnitude alone does not establish a reasoning benefit; inspect the paired control table and raw traces.

      Research Limitations & Next Checks
      
    What Remains Before Stronger Research Claims
    
      
    
        All core infrastructure, exact adapter equivalence tests, 12-method control suites, and pilot/test
        validation runs are recorded, but larger independent and compute-matched evaluation remains before a paper-level claim. The remaining work is:
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    
        
    Step 1
    
        
    Scale Independent Evaluation
    
        
    
          Expand beyond the 18 distinct problems per controller/fresh recording. Three seeds per problem are repeated measurements, not extra independent problems.
          Choose the sample size from a preregistered power analysis, then report problem-cluster uncertainty and compute-matched comparisons.
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Step 2
    
        
    Margin-Proportional $\beta(m_t^A, m_t^B)$ Damping
    
        
    
          Replace hard binary gating in $E_2$ with smooth confidence weighting $\beta_t = \beta_{\max} \cdot \sigma(m_t^{\mathrm{visit}} - m_t^A)$
          to prevent over-excursion on borderline-correct instances like arith_validation_003.
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Step 3
    
        
    Paper Generation & Vector Figures
    
        
    
          Generate publication-ready PDF/SVG figures from verified artifacts and compile the manuscript
          directly from reports/final_results.json, including theorem proofs and qualitative trace appendices.
